# Task: Probing out-of-distribution generalization in machine learning for materials

- Task ID: `T_P016_01`
- Canonical name: `probing_out_of_distribution_generalization`
- Paper: `P016`

## Objective

To systematically examine the performance of various ML methods across over 700 OOD tasks within large materials datasets and identify what constitutes a truly challenging OOD task.

## Material scope

- crystalline solids
- inorganic materials

## Target properties

- formation energy
- band gap
- bulk modulus

## Constraints

- test data must be statistically OOD (differing attribute distributions) but may or may not be representationally OOD

## Input requirements

- ab initio-derived materials databases (JARVIS, Materials Project, OQMD)
- materials representations (embeddings or descriptors)

## Output goal

- identification of representationally OOD vs. ID test data
- quantification of model generalization performance (MAE, R2) on OOD tasks
- analysis of scaling behavior (training time/size) on representationally OOD tasks

## Research stages

1. Selection of ab initio-derived materials databases (`data_acquisition`)
2. Definition of OOD tasks using heuristic criteria (`candidate_generation`)
3. Training of representative ML models on each OOD task (`model_training`)
4. Evaluation of OOD performance using MAE and R2 metrics (`model_evaluation`)
5. Representation space analysis using UMAP and kernel density estimation (`computational_validation`)
6. SHAP-based bias source identification for leave-one-element-out tasks (`candidate_screening`)
7. Learning curve analysis for training time and training set size scaling (`model_evaluation`)

## Dataset uses

- Selection of ab initio-derived materials databases: JARVIS-DFT (source, DU_P016_001)
- Selection of ab initio-derived materials databases: Materials Project (source, DU_P016_002)
- Selection of ab initio-derived materials databases: Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD) (source, DU_P016_003)

## Evidence

- P016, PDF page 1: "In this work, we conduct a systematic examination of the performance of various ML methods across over 700 OOD tasks within large materials datasets. These tasks are speciﬁcally designed to challenge common heuristics based on chemistry or structural symmetry."
- P016, PDF page 7, Discussion: "Our extensive OOD examinations show that existing energy models exhibit remarkable generalization capabilities to new chemical or structural groups outside the training set."
