## Goal

To select physically admissible, parameter-free theoretical relations for porosity dependence of sound velocities—including MMT, differential, exponential, self-consistent, percolation, and numerical benchmark relations—and screen them against experimental data.

## Input

- porosity φ
- Poisson’s ratio assumptions (ν0 ≈ 0.2 or 0.35)
- Equations 4–10

## Output

- predicted Vr(φ) curves for each model
- ranking of model fidelity for each material class

## Evidence

- P076, PDF page 10, CONCLUSION: "A comparison of the porosity dependence of the relative values of Young’s modulus and sound velocities shows that all relative Young’s modulus values are either close to or below (but never above) the exponential prediction, and none of them is significantly below the Pabst–Gregorová percolation relation or the Pabst–Uhlířová benchmark relation for overlapping monosized spheres."
- P076, PDF page 2, THEORETICAL: "Predictions for the porosity dependence of the sound velocity22 can be derived from the well-known predictive models for elastic properties, including the Maxwell–Mori–Tanaka (MMT) relation,23,24 the differential relation24,25 or Gibson–Ashby relation for open-cell foams,26,27 the (Pabst–Gregorová-type) exponential relation28,29 and the self-consistent model relation,24,29 as well as the numerical (Pabst–Uhlířová) benchmark relation for partially sintered materials with concave porosity (overlapping solid sphere model)30 and the one-parameter (Pabst–Gregorová) percolation relation,31"
