# Stage: comparison of TAPSim-MD results with experimental observations and traditional TAPSim

- Stage ID: `S_P097_06`
- Stage type: `model_evaluation`
- Stage order: 6
- Task: `T_P097_01` — interpreting field evaporation through dynamic bond-breaking processes

## Goal

Validate the physical fidelity of TAPSim-MD by demonstrating its ability to reproduce both experimental surface morphology (FIM) and reconstruction artifacts (mixed layers) without ad hoc assumptions.

## Input

- TAPSim-MD simulated reconstruction (Fig. 1f)
- experimental z-SDMs (Fig. 1e,h,j–l)
- TAPSim simulation (Fig. 1c)
- FIM images (Fig. 1b)

## Output

- quantitative agreement in interplanar spacing (0.9 Å shift)
- qualitative match in bilayer surface morphology
- reproduction of mixed-layer reconstruction regardless of experimental settings

## Datasets used

- Experimental z-SDM and AtomVicinity analyses from literature and own experiments — benchmark: Serve as benchmark for validating the physical fidelity of TAPSim-MD by demonstrating its ability to reproduce experimental surface morphology (FIM) and reconstruction artifacts (mixed layers) without ad hoc assumptions.

## Evidence

- P097, PDF page 4, Results and discussion: "The reconstruction result simulated by TAPSim-MD is shown in Fig. 1(f), where we find a distance of 0.9 Å between adjacent Ti and Al layers with Al positioned above Ti. The simulated reconstruction result resembles previously discussed experimental results of Ti50Al48Ru2 and Ti42Al48Nb10 in Fig. 1(e) and (h), where a shift of around 1 Å between adjacent Ti and Al layers is observed."
- P097, PDF page 4, Results and discussion: "The same pattern is observed in our TAPSim simulation with two different evaporation fields assigned to two atom types (Fig. 1(c)) and TAPSim-MD simulation (Fig. 1(d)). In the TAPSim simulation, atoms with a higher evaporation field form the top layer followed by a layer of low-evaporation field atoms where only small protrusions are exposed. In TAPSim-MD, the same pattern is found as well, however, there is no pre-defined evaporation field postulated at this time."
