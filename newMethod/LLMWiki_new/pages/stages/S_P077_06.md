## Goal

Validate the correlation between phase/microstructure and mechanical performance.

## Input

- hardness and fracture toughness data (Table 1, Table 3, Figure 7)
- microstructural observations (Figures 3–6, Table 2)

## Output

- confirmed structure–property relationships: fine α-grains → high hardness; elongated β-grains → high fracture toughness

## Datasets used

- None stated

## Evidence

- P077, PDF page 7, 3.4 Mechanical Properties: "The high hardness at lower temperatures is primarily attributed to the high content of α-Si3N4 phase and the refined equiaxed grain structure. The significant increase in fracture toughness when temperature rises or holding time extends is attributed to the increased content of β-Si3N4."
- P077, PDF page 1, ABSTRACT: "The highest hardness of 23.6 ± 0.4 GPa was obtained in the 1550-100-5 sample, while the highest fracture toughness of 8.4 ± 0.4 MPa ⋅m1/2 was achieved in the 1700-100-20 sample."
