# Stage: quantitative assessment of crystal generation

- Stage ID: `S_P102_05`
- Stage type: `model_evaluation`
- Stage order: 5
- Task: `T_P102_01` — crystal structure generation

## Goal

Evaluate the quality and novelty of the generated crystal structures using established metrics.

## Input

- generated crystal structures
- ground truth test set
- M3GNet energy calculator

## Output

- validity scores (structural/compositional)
- coverage scores (COV-R/COV-P)
- property distribution (Wasserstein distance)
- thermodynamic stability percentage

## Datasets used

- Materials Project — label_source: Provide ground truth materials for computing coverage metrics (COV-R and COV-P) to measure similarity between ensembles of generated materials and ground truth materials in the test set.
- American Mineralogist Crystal Structure Database — label_source: Provide ground truth materials for computing coverage metrics (COV-R and COV-P) to measure similarity between ensembles of generated materials and ground truth materials in the test set.
- GNoME — label_source: Provide ground truth materials for computing coverage metrics (COV-R and COV-P) to measure similarity between ensembles of generated materials and ground truth materials in the test set.

## Evidence

- P102, PDF page 14, Methods: "To evaluate the performance of LLMs for crystal generation, we rely on the following metrics. 1. Validity check: Structural validity and compositional validity are calculated as described in [39]. The former indicates that the distance between the centres of two atoms is greater than the sum of their atomic radii. The compositional validity is obtained using SMACT[60], which identifies if the given material is charge neutral based on all possible charge combinations. 2. Coverage: We use two coverage metrics, COV-R (recall) and COV-P (precision), described in [39] to measure the similarity between ensembles of generated materials and ground truth materials in the test set. 3. Property statistics: We compute the Wasserstein distance between the property distributions of the generated materials and the test materials. 4. Stability Check: We used M3GNet ([61]) to approximate force, energy, and stress in crystal unit cells."
