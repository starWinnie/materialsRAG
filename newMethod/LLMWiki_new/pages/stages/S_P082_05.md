# Stage: Correlation of surface topology with mechanical measurements

- Stage ID: `S_P082_05`
- Stage type: `computational_validation`
- Stage order: 5
- Task: `T_P082_01` — Understanding the fiber–matrix interface properties governing the fiber-direction compressive strength improvement of the hybrid HM CFRPs

## Goal

To link observed surface topography differences (grooved vs. smooth) to the measured 48% difference in interface friction and its contribution to the 29 MPa IFSS difference.

## Input

- SEM surface images (Figure 2)
- Quantified interface friction and IFSS values

## Output

- Mechanistic explanation: Surface roughness of IM fibers increases micromechanical interlocking and interfacial friction, contributing substantially to interface shear strength

## Datasets used

- None stated

## Evidence

- P082, PDF page 8, 6. Results: "The 18 MPa difference in the interface friction between IM and HM fibers appears to have a major contribution to the 29 MPa IFSS difference between IM and HM fibers. In particular, a substantial difference in fiber surface topology, as illustrated in Figure 2, with IM fibers exhibiting much more surface roughness compared to HM fibers, is likely to be an underlying cause."
- P082, PDF page 1, 7. Concluding Remarks: "Such experiments reveal an approximately 48% higher maximum shear traction due to interface friction for IM carbon fibers compared to HM fibers."
