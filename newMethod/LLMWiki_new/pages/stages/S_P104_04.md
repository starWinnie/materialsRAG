# Stage: Finite element modeling of sample thermal history during fast firing

- Stage ID: `S_P104_04`
- Stage type: `computational_validation`
- Stage order: 4
- Task: `T_P104_01` — Fast firing of 3 mol% yttria-stabilized zirconia: On the effect of heating rate on sintering

## Goal

Compute actual temperature evolution inside the pellet during fast firing to accurately calculate Θ(T,t) integral, accounting for furnace temperature drop and sample heating dynamics.

## Input

- furnace geometry
- MoSi2 heating element properties
- pellet thermal conductivity and specific heat
- COMSOL Multiphysics

## Output

- FEM-simulated temperature curve at pellet center
- corrected Θ(T,t) values for FF experiments

## Datasets used

- FEM-simulated temperature evolution inside 3YSZ pellet during fast firing — computational_validation: To compute actual temperature evolution inside the pellet during fast firing for accurate Θ(T,t) integral calculation.

## Evidence

- P104, PDF page 6, 3.3 Fast firing versus master sintering curve: "Two finite elements method (FEM) simulations were performed using the COMSOL multiphysics software... The time needed to heat up the sample as it were introduced in the hot furnace at the equilibrium is in the order of ≈7 s according to FEM (Figure 4B). On the other hand, when opening the furnace to introduce the pellets for FF the furnace temperature immediately dropped ≈40◦C and reach again the nominal value in ≈1–1.5 min. Hence, we can assume that the actual sample permanence at the firing temperature for the 10 min FF experiment was ≈9 min."
