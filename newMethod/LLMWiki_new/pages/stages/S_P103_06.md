# Stage: In-situ EBSD and TEM analysis to confirm <c + a> slip activation and interfacial slip transfer

- Stage ID: `S_P103_06`
- Stage type: `computational_validation`
- Stage order: 6
- Task: `T_P103_01` — Design of high-strength, high-uniform-elongation α–β titanium alloys resistant to oxygen embrittlement

## Goal

Validate the mechanistic basis of the design—activation of {11̅22}<c + a> slip and its transfer across α–β interfaces—using in-situ tensile testing coupled with electron microscopy.

## Input

- tensile specimens subjected to in-situ SEM/EBSD and TEM
- EBSD phase maps, TEM g·b analysis, KAM mapping

## Output

- slip system activation percentages (e.g., 52% {11̅22}<c + a>)
- slip transfer statistics (e.g., 57% of events mediated by {11̅22}<c + a>)
- geometric compatibility factors (m’ = 0.8–1.0)

## Datasets used

- Ti-0.45O-4Fe alloy — computational_validation: In-situ EBSD and TEM analysis to confirm <c + a> slip activation and interfacial slip transfer
- Ti-0.5O-5Fe alloy — computational_validation: In-situ EBSD and TEM analysis to confirm <c + a> slip activation and interfacial slip transfer

## Evidence

- P103, PDF page 5, Results and discussion: "To understand the advanced uniform plasticity in high-oxygen Ti-0.45O-4Fe alloy, we performed in-situ EBSD tensile testing, focusing on the uniform deformation stage (Fig. 5a and Supplementary Fig. 18). The analysis revealed activation of both prismatic and pyramidal slip (Supplementary Fig. 19), with a distribution of 20% for {10̅10}<a>, 24% for {10̅11}<a>, and 52% for {11̅22}<c + a>."
- P103, PDF page 5, Results and discussion: "TEM analysis of the Ti-0.45O-4Fe alloy annealed at 800 °C for 1 h and tested to 14.2% uniform elongation revealed abundant dislocations in both α and β phases within the uniformly deformed region (Fig. 5c–f) and the necking zone (Supplementary Fig. 21). Two-beam TEM g·b analysis confirmed the predominance of <c + a> dislocations in the deformed α phase and revealed distinct slip traces traversing α–β interfaces (Fig. 6), validating our <c + a>-dislocation-centered design strategy."
