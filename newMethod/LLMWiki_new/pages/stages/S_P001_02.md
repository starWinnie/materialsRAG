## Goal

represent crystal structures as graphs with node and edge features

## Input

- crystal structure M = (A, P, L)
- cutoff radius

## Output

- node features h(0)_i
- edge features e_ij

## Evidence

- P001, PDF page 4, Crystal Structures: "Crystal Structures Crystal structures can be described using a fundamental unit, such as a prim- itive cell, conventional unit cell, Bravais lattice, or asymmetric unit, together with its associated atoms. The unit is defined by a 3 × 3 lattice matrix that specifies its geometry in real space. Formally, a crystal structure is represented as M = (A, P, L), where A denotes atomic types, P = [p1, . . . , pN] ∈R3×N contains the fractional or Cartesian coordinates of the N atoms, and L = [ℓ1, ℓ2, ℓ3] ∈R3×3 defines the periodic lattice in real space."
- P001, PDF page 5, Graph embedding: "Graph embedding A crystal structure is represented as a graph G = (V, E), where vertices V = {v1, v2, . . . , vN} correspond to atoms one-hot encoded attributes (Xie & Grossman, 2018) and edges E ⊆V × V encode atomic interactions within a cutoff radius. Each atom i is characterized by a node feature h(0) i = Embed(vi) ∈Rd and coordinates ri = (xi, yi, zi) ∈R3. Edge features between atoms i and j are defined as: eij = RBF(dij) ⊕SBF(θijk) ⊕dij, dij = ∥ri −rj∥, (6) where RBF(·) denotes a radial basis function, SBF(·) a spherical basis function (Gasteiger et al.), θijk is the angle between bond ij and bond ik, and ⊕indicates concatenation."
