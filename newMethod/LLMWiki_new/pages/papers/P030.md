## Research summary

The paper proposes a machine learning method to predict the charge density of atomic systems—core quantum mechanical quantity from which all ground-state physicochemical properties derive—with both high accuracy and computational efficiency. It introduces three key ingredients: (1) representing charge density using atomic and virtual orbitals (spherical fields centered at atoms and bond midpoints), (2) using expressive, domain-informed, and learnable even-tempered Gaussian basis sets, and (3) employing a high-capacity SE(3)-equivariant neural network architecture (eSCN). The method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on the QM9 charge density benchmark while being >30× faster than prior approaches, enabling flexible accuracy–efficiency trade-offs.
