## Research summary

Carbon stoichiometry regulation was employed to regulate the microstructure and properties of (Ti0.25 Ta0.25 Nb0.25 V0.25 )Cx ( x = 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2)-SiC high-entropy ceramic composites. Carbon-deficient and carbon-rich high-entropy carbide (HEC) powders were synthesized by a molten salt-assisted method and combined with 20 vol.% silicon carbide (SiC) for spark plasma sintering. The results demonstrate that carbon vacancies significantly enhance atomic diffusion and densification behavior. HEC0.8 -SiC exhibits the lowest sintering activation energy (572–578 kJ mol− 1 ) and achieves a high relative density of 99.82%. Benefiting from refined grains, high densification, and vacancy-induced lattice distortion, HEC0.8 -SiC delivers excellent mechanical properties with a hardness of 17.86 GPa, strength of 484.6 MPa, and fracture toughness of 4.6 MPa ⋅m1/2 . Moreover, optimized carbon vacancies promote the formation of protective V-rich oxide scales and enhance radiation tolerance by suppressing defect migration and accumulation. This work provides a vacancy-engineering strategy for designing multifunctional high-entropy ceramic composites for extreme environments.
