## Research summary

This paper introduces LLM-Prop, a method that leverages large language models (LLMs) to predict properties of crystalline materials from their natural language text descriptions—generated by Robocrystallographer from CIF files—rather than from graph or structural representations. It proposes a fine-tuned T5 encoder architecture with specific preprocessing (e.g., [NUM]/[ANG] tokenization, [CLS] prepending, stopword removal) and label normalization strategies. The work benchmarks performance on six crystal properties (band gap, formation energy per atom, energy above hull, unit cell volume, energy per atom, direct/indirect band gap classification), demonstrates superiority over state-of-the-art GNNs and MatBERT, releases the TextEdge benchmark dataset, and analyzes ablation effects of design choices.
