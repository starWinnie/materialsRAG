# Paper: Effect of Cross-Link Homogeneity on the High-Strain Behavior of Elastic Polymer Networks

## Research summary

This paper investigates how cross-link homogeneity (random vs. regular network architecture) affects the high-strain mechanical behavior—specifically the onset of strain stiffening—of poly(n-butyl acrylate) networks, using a double-network strategy to overcome premature fracture. It synthesizes and compares randomly cross-linked networks (via free-radical polymerization) and regularly cross-linked networks (via thiol-bromine click coupling of tetrafunctional star polymers), then swells both in monomer/cross-linker and polymerizes to form double networks for uniaxial tensile testing. Results show that, for networks with the same modulus, regular networks exhibit delayed strain stiffening relative to random ones, indicating that low- and high-strain responses arise from distinct molecular-scale features (defect content vs. elastically effective strand length).
