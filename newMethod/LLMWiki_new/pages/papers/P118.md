## Research summary

This study investigates the use of an Ag precursor layer to enable low-temperature (350 °C) growth of Cu(In,Ga)Se2 (CIGS) solar cell absorbers using the conventional three-stage deposition process, targeting bifacial and tandem photovoltaic applications. The authors first optimize Ag precursor thickness (5, 10, 25 nm) at a high-temperature reference condition (600 °C), balancing improved film quality against reduced net acceptor density. They identify 10 nm as optimal and then transfer this condition to low-temperature deposition (350 °C). Characterization shows Ag incorporation improves minority-carrier lifetime, diode parameters, and Urbach energy at 350 °C, enabling ACIGS solar cells to maintain ~15% power conversion efficiency, whereas non-Ag CIGS devices degrade severely.
