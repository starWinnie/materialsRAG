## Research summary

A systematic compositional matrix of twelve as-cast alloys (13.2–30.8 wt% Cr; 1.95–3.96 wt% C) was designed to elucidate the combined influence of chromium-to-carbon ratio (Cr/C = 4.19–15.32) and carbide volume fraction (22.5–44.2%) on microstructure and multi-property response. Thermodynamic modelling integrated with real-time thermal analysis shows that non-equilibrium solidification underpins matrix constitution and carbide evolution, with pronounced deviations from equilibrium predictions. X-ray diffraction and microscopy confirm M7C3 as the dominant carbide with a Cr/C-dependent transition between austenitic and martensitic matrices driven by chromium partitioning. Mechanical and tribological properties showed pronounced non-linearity, with an optimal compositional window (Cr/C = ~12; CVF = ~33%) maximising hardness, fracture toughness and three-body abrasion resistance. Corrosion resistance correlates mainly with matrix chromium availability, revealing a trade-off between wear and electrochemical performance. The results establish Cr/C ratio and carbide fraction as predictive design variables for engineering optimisation of as-cast HCWCIs in demanding service environments.
