# Paper: Effect of Fiber–Matrix Interface Friction on Compressive Strength of High-Modulus Carbon Composites

- Paper ID: `P082`
- Year: 2023
- DOI: 10.3390/molecules28052049
- File: `224_molecules-28-02049.pdf`
- Extraction status: completed

## Research summary

This work investigates why intermediate-modulus (IM) carbon fibers exhibit stronger fiber–matrix interface strength than high-modulus (HM) carbon fibers in hybrid carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP) composites, focusing on the role of interface friction arising from surface topology differences. Using SEM imaging and in situ SEM-based push-out and push-back experiments, the authors measure and compare interface shear strength (IFSS) and interface friction strength for IM and HM fibers in a nanosilica-toughened hybrid CFRP, revealing ~48% higher interface friction for IM fibers—attributed to their rougher, grooved surface versus the smoother HM fiber surface—and linking this difference to improved compressive strength.
