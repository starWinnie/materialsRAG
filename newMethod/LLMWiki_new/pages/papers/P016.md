## Research summary

This paper systematically evaluates the out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization capabilities of various machine learning models across over 700 OOD tasks derived from three large ab initio materials databases (JARVIS, Materials Project, OQMD). It challenges the validity of heuristic-based OOD task definitions (e.g., leave-one-element-out, leave-one-space-group-out) by showing that most such tasks are not truly challenging—test data often reside within the representational domain of the training data (i.e., interpolation), not outside it (i.e., extrapolation). The study reveals that robust OOD performance is common even for simple models like XGBoost, and that neural scaling laws (improvement with larger training sets or longer training) fail for representationally OOD tasks, sometimes worsening performance. It introduces a representation-space-based method (using UMAP and kernel density estimation) to distinguish statistically OOD from representationally OOD test data, arguing that only the latter constitute genuinely challenging benchmarks.
