## Research summary

This work uses a machine learning potential (DP_SCAN) with SCAN functional accuracy to systematically investigate how cation doping and oxygen vacancy-mediated non-stoichiometry regulate the melting point of HfO2, predicting that optimized HfO1.85 achieves a melting point of 3042°C—exceeding the 3000°C barrier—and establishing a unified three-factor framework (synergistic bond strengthening, bond distribution uniformity, charge-compensating defect compatibility) to explain melting point regulation mechanisms.
