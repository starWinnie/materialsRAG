# Task: Porosity dependence of sound velocities in partially sintered ceramics

- Task ID: `T_P076_01`
- Canonical name: `porosity_dependence_of_sound_velocities_in_partially_sintered_ceramics`
- Paper: `P076`

## Objective

To determine and compare the porosity dependence of sound velocities (and related elastic properties) in partially sintered ceramics and assess the validity of theoretical model predictions.

## Material scope

- alumina
- zirconia
- ATZ
- mullite

## Target properties

- sound velocities
- Young's modulus
- shear modulus
- Poisson's ratio

## Constraints

- partially sintered microstructures
- concave pores between convex grains
- statistically isotropic microstructures

## Input requirements

- porosity (φ)
- theoretical density (ρ0)
- dense-material elastic constants (E0, G0, ν0, Vt0, Vl0)

## Output goal

- relative sound velocities (Vtr, Vlr)
- relative Young’s modulus (Er)
- VL/VT ratios
- model–data agreement assessment

## Research stages

1. Preparation and characterization of partially sintered ceramic samples (`data_acquisition`)
2. Measurement of sound velocities and Young’s modulus (`experimental_validation`)
3. Calculation of relative sound velocities and elastic constants (`label_generation`)
4. Selection and comparison of theoretical model predictions (`candidate_screening`)
5. Model–data comparison and correlation analysis (`computational_validation`)

## Dataset uses

- None stated

## Evidence

- P076, PDF page 1, Abstract: "Porosity dependence of sound velocities in partially sintered alumina, zirconia, ATZ, and mullite ceramics"
- P076, PDF page 2, THEORETICAL: "The present work attempts to correct this basic misunderstanding by comparing the few admissible parameter-free relations that are available today for predicting the porosity dependence of sound velocities with recent experimental data obtained in our laboratory for a few chemically simple ceramics (alumina, zirconia, alumina–zirconia composites, and mullite ceramics) prepared by partial sintering to attain different levels of porosity."
