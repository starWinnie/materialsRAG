# Task: interpreting field evaporation through dynamic bond-breaking processes

- Task ID: `T_P097_01`
- Canonical name: `interpret_field_evaporation_via_dynamic_bond_breaking`
- Paper: `P097`

## Objective

Explain the disrupted evaporation sequence (Ti evaporating before Al) in [001]-oriented γ-TiAl by uncovering the underlying atomic-scale dynamic mechanism, not thermodynamic binding energies.

## Material scope

- γ-TiAl
- TiAl intermetallic compound
- [001]-oriented stoichiometric, unalloyed TiAl

## Target properties

- field evaporation sequence
- evaporation field
- bond-breaking process
- atom trajectories
- surface morphology

## Constraints

- no prior knowledge of charge states
- no assumptions about evaporation fields
- must reproduce experimental bilayer surface morphology and mixed-layer reconstruction

## Input requirements

- [001]-oriented crystalline tip geometry
- well-fitted interatomic potential (EAM by Zope and Mishin)
- electrostatic force calculation via Poisson solver
- molecular dynamics framework

## Output goal

- ab initio prediction of evaporation events and atom trajectories
- explanation of preferential Ti evaporation via two-step bond breaking
- reproduction of experimental FIM surface morphology and APT reconstruction artifacts

## Research stages

1. experimental APT specimen preparation and data collection (`data_acquisition`)
2. construction of [001]-oriented virtual TiAl crystalline tip (`data_preparation`)
3. TAPSim-MD simulation execution with electrostatic voltage ramping (`model_training`)
4. analysis of atom trajectories and bond-breaking dynamics (`computational_validation`)
5. deterministic validation using simplified model system (`computational_validation`)
6. comparison of TAPSim-MD results with experimental observations and traditional TAPSim (`model_evaluation`)

## Dataset uses

- experimental APT specimen preparation and data collection: Experimental APT dataset from Ti-52Al-2Nb (at.%) ingot (experimental_validation, DU_P097_001)
- construction of [001]-oriented virtual TiAl crystalline tip: TAPSim-MD virtual [001]-oriented TiAl crystalline tip (source, DU_P097_002)
- TAPSim-MD simulation execution with electrostatic voltage ramping: TAPSim-MD virtual [001]-oriented TiAl crystalline tip (training, DU_P097_003)
- analysis of atom trajectories and bond-breaking dynamics: TAPSim-MD virtual [001]-oriented TiAl crystalline tip (computational_validation, DU_P097_004)
- deterministic validation using simplified model system: TAPSim-MD simplified LAMMPS model system for deterministic analysis (computational_validation, DU_P097_005)
- comparison of TAPSim-MD results with experimental observations and traditional TAPSim: Experimental z-SDM and AtomVicinity analyses from literature and own experiments (benchmark, DU_P097_006)

## Evidence

- P097, PDF page 7, Conclusions: "We have revisited with experiments and simulations the disrupted field evaporation sequence of [001]-oriented γ-TiAl."
- P097, PDF page 3, Methods: "In order to sort through these contradictory experimental and theoretical findings, we revisit here the field evaporation sequence of [001]-oriented stoichiometric, unalloyed TiAl as a starting point by applying our recently introduced full-dynamics field evaporation simulation approach TAPSim-MD [13]."
- P097, PDF page 1, ABSTRACT: "This effect has commonly been attributed to the lower evaporation field of Ti compared to Al. Instead, the seemingly counterintuitive ‘‘preferential’’ evaporation of the stronger-bonded Ti atoms can be explained by a two-step bond-breaking process where each bond-breaking step requires a smaller external force than that needed for Al for which evaporation happens by simultaneously breaking all bonds."
