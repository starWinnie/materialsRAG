# Task: Predicting thermodynamic stability of inorganic compounds

- Task ID: `T_P005_01`
- Canonical name: `predict_thermodynamic_stability_of_inorganic_compounds`
- Paper: `P005`

## Objective

Accurately predict whether an inorganic compound is thermodynamically stable (i.e., has decomposition energy ΔHd ≤ 0 meV/atom or ≤ 25–40 meV/atom under relaxed thresholds)

## Material scope

- inorganic compounds
- perovskite halides
- Li-containing oxides
- transition metal oxides
- two-dimensional materials
- double perovskite oxides

## Target properties

- thermodynamic stability
- decomposition energy (ΔHd)

## Constraints

- must work with compositional input only (no structure required)
- must generalize to unexplored composition space
- must achieve high sample efficiency

## Input requirements

- chemical formula
- elemental composition
- electron configuration of constituent elements

## Output goal

- binary classification: stable vs unstable
- continuous regression output for ΔHd

## Research stages

1. Collecting DFT-computed thermodynamic stability data from public databases (`data_acquisition`)
2. Preprocessing compositional data and encoding electron configurations (`data_preparation`)
3. Assigning stability labels based on DFT-derived decomposition energy thresholds (`label_generation`)
4. Training base-level models (Magpie, Roost, ECCNN) and meta-level model via stacked generalization (`model_training`)
5. Benchmarking predictive performance across multiple metrics and datasets (`model_evaluation`)
6. Enumerating candidate compounds in unexplored composition space (e.g., perovskite oxides) (`candidate_generation`)
7. Applying trained ECSG model to rank and filter candidate compounds by predicted stability probability (`candidate_screening`)
8. Validating top candidates via first-principles DFT calculations (`computational_validation`)
