# Task: Design of high-strength, high-uniform-elongation α–β titanium alloys resistant to oxygen embrittlement

- Task ID: `T_P103_01`
- Canonical name: `design_high_strength_high_uniform_elongation_alpha_beta_titanium_alloys`
- Paper: `P103`

## Objective

Simultaneously address the yield strength–uniform elongation trade-off and oxygen embrittlement in α–β titanium alloys by transforming high oxygen from an embrittling liability into a performance enabler.

## Material scope

- α–β titanium alloys
- Ti–O–Fe system

## Target properties

- high yield strength (σ0.2 ≥ 980 MPa)
- high uniform elongation (εUEL ≥ 14%)
- high total elongation (εf ≥ 27%)
- sustained pyramidal <c + a> slip activation and transfer

## Constraints

- oxygen content ≥ 0.40% to activate pyramidal <c + a> slip
- α-lamellar thickness of 1–3 μm to balance strength and deformation compatibility
- β-phase volume fraction ~20%
- coherent α–β interfaces
- low initial dislocation density

## Input requirements

- high oxygen content (≥0.40 wt.% O)
- Fe as β-stabilizer (4–5 wt.% Fe)
- laser-based powder bed fusion (PBF-LB) processing
- post-PBF-LB annealing

## Output goal

- Ti-(0.43–0.46)O-(3.91–3.99)Fe and Ti-(0.49–0.50)O-(4.96–5.00)Fe alloys with ≥14% uniform elongation at ≥980 MPa yield strength and ≥13% uniform elongation at ≥1075 MPa yield strength

## Research stages

1. Alloy composition design targeting oxygen-mediated <c + a> slip activation and β-phase-mediated slip transfer (`candidate_generation`)
2. Production of gas-atomized spherical Ti-O-Fe powder (`data_acquisition`)
3. PBF-LB fabrication of α–β Ti-O-Fe specimens (`candidate_generation`)
4. Post-PBF-LB annealing to optimize microstructure and eliminate metastable phases (`candidate_screening`)
5. Quasi-static tensile testing to evaluate mechanical properties (`experimental_validation`)
6. In-situ EBSD and TEM analysis to confirm <c + a> slip activation and interfacial slip transfer (`computational_validation`)

## Evidence

- P103, PDF page 1: "Here, we present a dual strategy for α–β titanium alloys that transforms high oxygen from an embrittling liability into a powerful performance enabler. First, we leverage high oxygen ( ≥0.40%) to activate prominent pyramidal <c + a> slip in the α-phase. Second, we engineer a tailored α–β microstructure through alloy design (Ti–O–Fe), laser-based powder bed fusion, and annealing to enable sustained slip transfer across α–β interfaces."
- P103, PDF page 1: "This study presents a dual design strategy to address the persistent yield strength–uniform elongation trade-off in α-β Ti alloys—the workhorse of the titanium industry30. Successfully resolving this challenge could open promising avenues for obtaining high fracture toughness, as predicted by established correlations with tensile properties37. Notably, our conceptual framework also unexpectedly resolves another longstanding challenge: the embrittling effect of high oxygen content."
- P103, PDF page 7: "We have demonstrated a framework that overcomes the long-standing trade-off between yield strength and uniform elongation in α–β titanium alloys through two key innovations: (i) leveraging high oxygen content (≥0.40%) to activate pyramidal <c + a> slip in α-Ti, and (ii) engineering an α–β lamellar microstructure via integrated compositional and processing design to sustain slip transfer across phase boundaries."
