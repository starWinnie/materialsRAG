# Task: Synergetic Improvement of Interfacial Performance and Impact Resistance of Carbon Fiber-Reinforced Epoxy Composite via Continuous Electrochemical Oxidation

- Task ID: `T_P096_01`
- Canonical name: `improve_interfacial_performance_and_impact_resistance_of_cf_ep_composite`
- Paper: `P096`

## Objective

Synergetic improvement of the interfacial performance and impact resistance of carbon fiber-reinforced epoxy (CF/EP) composites

## Material scope

- carbon fiber-reinforced epoxy composite
- T800H-grade carbon fiber
- epoxy resin

## Target properties

- interfacial shear strength (IFSS)
- impact resistance
- compressive strength after impact (CAI)
- damage area after impact

## Constraints

- maintain or improve static mechanical properties
- avoid fiber fracture-induced degradation of compressive strength after impact
- industrial scalability via continuous production line

## Input requirements

- T800H-grade carbon fiber
- epoxy resin
- NH4HCO3 solution (10 wt%) as electrolyte
- continuous electrochemical oxidation setup

## Output goal

- optimized CF/EP composite with maximized CAI (331 MPa) and minimized damage area (286 mm2) while preserving fiber tensile strength

## Research stages

1. Electrochemical treatment of carbon fibers at varying current densities (`candidate_generation`)
2. Surface physicochemical characterization of treated carbon fibers (`data_acquisition`)
3. Monofilament tensile testing of treated carbon fibers (`data_acquisition`)
4. Interfacial shear strength (IFSS) testing (`data_acquisition`)
5. Low-speed impact and compression testing of CF/EP laminates (`data_acquisition`)
6. Analysis of structure–property relationships to identify optimal current density (`candidate_screening`)

## Dataset uses

- None stated

## Evidence

- P096, PDF page 1, Introduction: "This study elucidated the synergistic improvement of the interfacial performance and impact resistance of carbon fiber-reinforced epoxy (CF/EP) composites by employing continuous electrochemical oxidation."
- P096, PDF page 1, Abstract: "After electrochemical oxidation, the IFSS of the CF/EP increased by 48.8%, significantly improving the impact resistance of the composites."
- P096, PDF page 1, Abstract: "The experimental results showed that, when the current density reached 0.15 mA/cm2, the damage area after impact reduced by 61%. Concurrently, fiber fracture and resin failure became the primary energy-dissipation modes, maximizing the fiber reinforcement effect and enhancing the impact resistance. However, fiber fracture deteriorated the static mechanical properties of the composites. Subsequently, at a 0.10 mA/cm2 current density, the CF/EP composites exhibited an increased compressive strength after an impact of 331 MPa."
