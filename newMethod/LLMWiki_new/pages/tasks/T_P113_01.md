# Task: Solid Solution in BaS–La2S3 Ceramics Produced by Mechanosynthesis and Their Potential for Rare-Earth Doping

- Task ID: `T_P113_01`
- Canonical name: `develop_bas_la2s3_solid_solution_ceramics_for_rare_earth_doping`
- Paper: `P113`

## Objective

To develop BaS–La2S3 ceramics exhibiting a continuous solid solution with tunable structural properties and capacity for rare-earth doping, enabling potential use as high-power infrared laser host materials.

## Material scope

- BaS
- La2S3
- BaLa2S4
- Dy2S3

## Target properties

- broad infrared transparency
- outstanding thermal stability
- continuous solid solution formation
- rare-earth ion solubility
- adherence to Vegard's law
- low phonon energy

## Constraints

- avoid hazardous sulfurization gases (e.g., H2S, CS2)
- avoid high-temperature synthesis causing sulfur loss
- maintain crystallinity to minimize concentration quenching
- achieve homogeneous rare-earth distribution

## Input requirements

- stoichiometric La2S3 and BaS powders
- inert atmosphere (Ar glovebox)
- high-energy ball mill with tungsten carbide media

## Output goal

- phase-pure, single-phase ceramic powders forming a continuous solid solution across x BaS–(1−x) La2S3 (x = 0–50)
- dopant-incorporated powders (e.g., Dy-doped) without secondary phases
- thermally stable precursor powders suitable for rapid sintering into bulk ceramics

## Research stages

1. Procurement of precursor powders (`data_acquisition`)
2. Mechanosynthesis of ceramic powders (`candidate_generation`)
3. X-ray diffraction analysis of as-milled and annealed powders (`model_evaluation`)
4. Vegard’s law validation and vacancy population modeling (`computational_validation`)
5. Thermal stability assessment via calorimetry (`candidate_screening`)
6. Rare-earth doping feasibility screening (`candidate_screening`)

## Evidence

- P113, PDF page 1, ABSTRACT: "BaS–La2 S3 ceramics exhibit broad infrared transparency and outstanding thermal stability, which makes them attractive candidate for optical windows in extreme environments among other technological applications. Precursor powders for these ceramics are commonly produced by sulfurization with gases that can be hazardous and costly. Instead, we present a simple low-temperature synthesis method based on high-energy ball-milling."
- P113, PDF page 2, Results: "In this study, we present a low-temperature route for the preparation of ceramic powders in the BaLa2 S4 –La2 S3 system through mechanosynthesis in a high-energy ball mill. This alleviates the need for high temperature reaction and the associated loss of sulfur. Such powders may then serve as precursors for the fabrication of bulk ceramics though rapid sintering."
- P113, PDF page 4, Rare Earth Solubility: "The presence of cationic vacancies in the BaS–La2 S3 system presents interesting possibilities for solubility of other species. In particular, the solubility of rare-earths is of much interest for the fabrication of active phases with luminescent properties in the IR."
