# Task: Constructing high-polarization silica for polyimide-based nanocomposites with enhanced dielectric permittivity and mechanical performances

- Task ID: `T_P078_01`
- Canonical name: `construct_high_polarization_silica_for_pi_nanocomposites`
- Paper: `P078`

## Objective

To design and evaluate interfacial modifications of SiO2 nanoparticles in polyimide matrix that simultaneously enhance dielectric permittivity and mechanical performance

## Material scope

- polyimide/SiO2 nanocomposites
- silica nanoparticles
- silane coupling agents

## Target properties

- static dielectric permittivity (εs)
- Young modulus
- Shear modulus
- glass transition temperature (Tg)

## Constraints

- simultaneous enhancement of dielectric and mechanical properties
- interfacial atomic-scale control

## Input requirements

- silane coupling agents with specific functional groups (PhCOOH, NH2, Ph, PhNH2, PhCOCH3)
- polyimide chains
- SiO2 nanoparticles

## Output goal

- optimized interfacial structure with PhCOOH group that yields highest εs (6.66) and Young modulus (5.09 GPa)

## Research stages

1. Building molecular models of SiO2 nanoparticles and polyimide chains (`data_acquisition`)
2. Grafting silane coupling agents onto SiO2 surface (`data_preparation`)
3. Constructing and optimizing periodic PI/SiO2 composite models (`model_training`)
4. Simulating and quantifying structural, dynamic, and property metrics (`model_evaluation`)
5. Comparing functional group effects on dielectric and mechanical properties (`candidate_screening`)

## Dataset uses

- Building molecular models of SiO2 nanoparticles and polyimide chains: bulk α-quartz (source, DU_P078_001)
- Building molecular models of SiO2 nanoparticles and polyimide chains: 4,40-diamino diphenyl ether and pyromellitic dianhydride repeat unit (source, DU_P078_002)

## Evidence

- P078, PDF page 1, Abstract: "In this work, the silane coupling agents with 4-carboxyphenyl ( PhCOOH), amino, phenyl, 4-aminophenyl and 4-phenylmethylketone were employed to modify the interfaces of the polyimide/SiO2 (PIS) nanocomposites, and the effects of the interfacial structures on the dielectric and the mechanical properties for the PI-based composites were predicted by molecular dynamics simulation."
