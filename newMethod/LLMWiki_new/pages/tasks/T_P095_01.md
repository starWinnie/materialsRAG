# Task: Investigate the effect of cross-link homogeneity on the high-strain behavior of elastic polymer networks

- Task ID: `T_P095_01`
- Canonical name: `investigate_cross_link_homogeneity_effect_on_high_strain_behavior`
- Paper: `P095`

## Objective

To probe the role of cross-link heterogeneity and topological defects in the high-strain regime, specifically the onset of strain stiffening, in elastic polymer networks.

## Material scope

- poly(n-butyl acrylate) networks

## Target properties

- onset of strain stiffening
- modulus
- extensibility
- stress−strain response at high strain

## Constraints

- premature network fracture
- same backbone chemistry for fair comparison
- accessing high-strain regime without fracture

## Input requirements

- randomly cross-linked poly(n-butyl acrylate) single networks
- regularly cross-linked poly(n-butyl acrylate) single networks
- monomer/cross-linker mixture (n-butyl acrylate/hexanediol diacrylate)

## Output goal

- quantified comparison of strain stiffening onset (e.g., via fitted n values from eight-chain model) between random and regular double networks with matched modulus or target Mx

## Research stages

1. Synthesis of randomly cross-linked single networks (`data_acquisition`)
2. Synthesis of tetrafunctional star polymers (`data_acquisition`)
3. Synthesis of regularly cross-linked single networks (`data_acquisition`)
4. Preparation of double networks (`candidate_generation`)
5. Fitting stress−strain data to swelling-modified eight-chain model (`model_evaluation`)
6. Uniaxial tensile testing (`experimental_validation`)

## Dataset uses

- None stated

## Evidence

- P095, PDF page 1, ABSTRACT: "Here, we address this problem by using a double-network approach to investigate the high-strain behavior of both randomly and regularly cross-linked networks with the same backbone chemistry."
- P095, PDF page 2: "Here, we combine these two approaches to investigate the effect of cross-link homogeneity on the high-strain behavior of randomly and regularly cross-linked networks with the same backbone chemistry and test the hypothesis that regular networks should exhibit delayed strain stiffening relative to random networks with the same cross-link density."
