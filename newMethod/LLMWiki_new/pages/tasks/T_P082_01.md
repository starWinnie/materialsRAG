# Task: Understanding the fiber–matrix interface properties governing the fiber-direction compressive strength improvement of the hybrid HM CFRPs

- Task ID: `T_P082_01`
- Canonical name: `understand_fiber_matrix_interface_properties_governing_compressive_strength_improvement`
- Paper: `P082`

## Objective

To determine why IM carbon fibers have a stronger fiber–matrix interface compared to HM carbon fibers, specifically investigating interface friction as a mechanism driving interface shear strength improvement in hybrid HM CFRPs.

## Material scope

- high-modulus carbon fiber
- intermediate-modulus carbon fiber
- nanosilica-toughened epoxy matrix
- hybrid carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP)

## Target properties

- fiber–matrix interface friction strength
- fiber–matrix interface shear strength
- fiber surface topology

## Constraints

- All fibers are PAN-based
- Filament-level hybridization required
- Specimen thickness constrained to 20–30 μm for in situ SEM testing

## Input requirements

- Hybrid CFRP sample containing IM and HM carbon fibers
- SEM-capable nanomechanical load frame
- Flat-end diamond indenter tip
- Optical microscopy for fiber identification

## Output goal

- Quantified comparison of interface friction strength between IM and HM fibers
- Correlation of surface topology differences (grooved vs. smooth) with interface friction and shear strength
- Mechanistic explanation for compressive strength improvement in hybrid HM CFRPs

## Research stages

1. Fiber surface characterization using SEM (`data_acquisition`)
2. Hypothesis generation on interface friction mechanism (`candidate_generation`)
3. In situ SEM-based fiber push-out and push-back experiments (`experimental_validation`)
4. Comparison and statistical analysis of IFSS and interface friction strength (`model_evaluation`)
5. Correlation of surface topology with mechanical measurements (`computational_validation`)
