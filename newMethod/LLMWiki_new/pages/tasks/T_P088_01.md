# Task: engineering optimisation of as-cast HCWCIs

- Task ID: `T_P088_01`
- Canonical name: `engineering_optimisation_of_as_cast_hcwci`
- Paper: `P088`

## Objective

establish Cr/C ratio and carbide volume fraction as predictive design variables for engineering optimisation of as-cast HCWCIs in demanding service environments

## Material scope

- high chromium white cast iron (HCWCI)
- as-cast HCWCI alloys

## Target properties

- hardness
- fracture toughness
- three-body abrasion resistance
- corrosion resistance

## Constraints

- abrasion-corrosion trade-off
- non-equilibrium solidification
- as-cast condition (no post-casting heat treatment)

## Input requirements

- chromium-to-carbon ratio (Cr/C)
- carbide volume fraction (%CVF)

## Output goal

- predictive compositional windows for application-specific HCWCI design
- optimal compositional window (Cr/C = ~12; CVF = ~33%)

## Research stages

1. design and production of twelve as-cast HCWCI alloys (`data_acquisition`)
2. thermodynamic modelling using Thermo-Calc (`computational_validation`)
3. real-time thermal analysis using ATAS (`experimental_validation`)
4. metallographic sample preparation (`data_preparation`)
5. phase identification and microstructural characterization (`candidate_generation`)
6. multi-property evaluation (hardness, fracture toughness, abrasion, corrosion) (`model_evaluation`)
7. analysis of compositional-microstructural-property relationships (`candidate_screening`)

## Dataset uses

- thermodynamic modelling using Thermo-Calc: TCFE8 Database (pretraining, DU_P088_002)

## Evidence

- P088, PDF page 2, Introduction: "The aim of this study is to establish a composition-solidification-partitioning-property framework across a broad Cr/C (4.19–15.32) and CVF (22.5–44.2%) design space. By integrating thermodynamic modelling with real-time thermal analysis and multi-property evaluation, the study resolves how chromium partitioning under non-equilibrium solidification governs the coupled optimisation of hardness, fracture toughness, abrasion resistance, and corrosion behaviour. The results define predictive compositional windows for application-specific HCWCI design."
