# Paper: Probing out-of-distribution generalization in machine learning for materials

- Paper ID: `P016`

- DOI: 10.1038/s43246-024-00731-w
- File: `16_OOD_Generalization_Materials.pdf`
- Extraction status: completed

## Research summary

This paper systematically evaluates the out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization capabilities of various machine learning models across over 700 OOD tasks derived from large materials datasets (JARVIS, Materials Project, OQMD). It challenges heuristic definitions of OOD tasks—based on chemistry (e.g., leave-one-element-out) or structural symmetry (e.g., leave-one-space-group-out)—by revealing that most such tasks are not truly extrapolative but rather represent interpolation within the high-dimensional materials representation space. Using UMAP-based domain identification and kernel-density estimation, the authors distinguish statistically OOD from representationally OOD test data. They find that robust OOD performance is common—even for simple models like XGBoost—and that poor performance is concentrated in a few cases (e.g., H, F, O compounds) where test data lie outside the training domain. Crucially, neural scaling laws (increasing training size/time improves performance) hold only for representationally in-domain OOD tasks; for representationally OOD tasks, scaling often degrades performance. The work concludes that current OOD benchmarks overestimate generalizability and calls for rigorously challenging, physically meaningful OOD benchmarks.
